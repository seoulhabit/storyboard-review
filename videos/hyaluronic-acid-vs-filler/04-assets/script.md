## HOOK — s01 · myth correction + three identities (must land within ~10s)

**KIMBERLY:** Your hyaluronic-acid serum and dermal filler share a name, but they do completely different jobs — the kind your body makes, the kind in your serum, and the kind a doctor injects. <!-- C1 nominal + thesis, per the brief's suggested opening direction, near-verbatim -->

## MISCONCEPTION — s02 · not filler in a bottle

**KIMBERLY:** And the mix-up is expensive. People buy a serum expecting the results of an injection, then wonder why nothing changed. A serum is not filler in a bottle. <!-- [GEN] states the video's central question as a claim, reused from v2 -->

## MECHANISM — s03–s07 · topical size, plumping, water, filler

**KIMBERLY:** Here's where the two versions really split — one stays a loose, flowing molecule. The other gets locked into a rigid mesh. <!-- [GEN] bridge line; cues the free-chains-vs-mesh contrast animation -->

**KIMBERLY:** In a serum, size decides almost everything. Large chains mostly stay near the surface, forming a hydrating film. Smaller ones may travel farther into the upper layers of skin. <!-- C5 · chip Skin Res Technol · 2015 · hedge "may" -->

**KIMBERLY:** So why does the bottle say plumping? Hydrated surface cells can temporarily make fine lines appear softer — that's hydration, not correction. <!-- C6 · chip Dermatoendocrinol · 2012 · hedges "temporarily"/"appear" carry into on-screen type per [K-3] -->

**KIMBERLY:** Here's the part the marketing skips. Hyaluronic acid binds water — it doesn't manufacture it. Think of it as a sponge, not a water factory. A complete formula still needs something else — an ingredient that seals that water in, so it doesn't evaporate away. <!-- C7 · chip ChemRxiv · 2023 · the one retained analogy -->

**KIMBERLY:** A filler is chemically different. Its chains are cross-linked into a gel that holds its own shape, once it's placed beneath the skin, where it can physically add volume. <!-- C8/C9 · chip J Cosmet Dermatol · 2024 -->

## HISTORY — s08 · one line, after the central explanation

**KIMBERLY:** The name goes back to nineteen thirty-four, when it was isolated from the clear jelly inside a cow's eye. <!-- C2 · chip J Biol Chem · 1934 · placed after mechanism, never in the opening -->

## APPLICATION — s09–s10 · do not inject yourself

**KIMBERLY:** Is a filler just serum with a needle? Not even close. Do not inject yourself. <!-- rhetorical aside in Kimberly's own voice, per req. 11; sets up the full-frame card -->

> **ON-SCREEN, full frame:** DO NOT INJECT YOURSELF. <!-- C10 · REWORDED from "Never inject a topical serum" to track FDA's own sentence · chip FDA · Dermal Fillers -->

**KIMBERLY:** That's the F D A's own wording, not ours. Dermal fillers are a medical procedure with real risks, including tissue death, vision loss and stroke. Choose a provider trained to perform the injection. <!-- C10, C11 · chip FDA · Dermal Fillers -->

## RECAP + END SCENE — s11 · three-part badges, concise close

**KIMBERLY:** Same ingredient family — different size, different structure, different location. The body's version is a resident, the serum's version is hydration, the filler's version is volume. Serum hydrates, filler adds volume — same name, different jobs. <!-- C12 editorial — three visual identities; badges are plain labels, not a fourth analogy -->

> **END CARD:** Serum hydrates. Filler adds volume. Same name, different jobs.
