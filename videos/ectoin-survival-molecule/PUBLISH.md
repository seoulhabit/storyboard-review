## Description

Hook line first: it is the search snippet, and it is the only part most people
see before deciding.

```
In salt lakes where almost nothing survives, bacteria build a molecule that
holds water around itself. Cosmetic chemists now put that molecule in
moisturisers — and the evidence for it is more interesting, and thinner, than
the marketing suggests.

This is the origin story and the mechanism, not a product roundup. Where the
research is strong I say so. Where it is small, industry-funded, or measuring
something other than what the label implies, I say that too.

CHAPTERS
0:00 A molecule invented by bacteria trying not to die
1:13 Why it is not just another hyaluronic acid
2:07 What it might actually do for skin
2:54 What the human evidence really says
4:03 How to read an ectoin label
4:52 Why K-beauty picked it up
5:15 The verdict

WHAT THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SHOWS
PubMed indexes 12 ectoine clinical trials in total (queried 2026-09-02). That
is a small base, and some of the work is tied to companies that make or sell
the ingredient — bitop AG, Merck KGaA and Kao Corporation all appear in the
author affiliations of papers cited here. That does not make the findings
wrong. It does mean they are not independent.

Two specifics worth stating plainly:
• The 104-woman study reported a subject PREFERENCE for the ectoin arm. It did
  not report instrumented advantages in moisturisation or skin appearance.
• The 65-patient eczema study found ectoin EQUIVALENT to its comparator and
  well tolerated over 28 days. Equivalent is not superior.

The routine advice in "How to read an ectoin label" is editorial — my reading,
not a citation. It is flagged as unsourced on screen for that reason.

SOURCES
Schwibbert et al. 2010, Environ Microbiol — Halomonas elongata genome (PMID 20849449)
Lentzen & Schwarz 2006, Appl Microbiol Biotechnol — extremolytes (PMID 16957893)
Yu et al. 2007, J Phys Chem B — preferential exclusion / hydration (PMID 17676787)
Sahle et al. 2018, Phys Chem Chem Phys — tetrahedral water order (PMID 30379162)
Graf et al. 2008, Clin Dermatol — barrier and TEWL (PMID 18691511)
Bow et al. 2021, Biochem Biophys Rep — keratin dispersion, SC hydration (PMID 34584987)
Heinrich et al. 2007, Skin Pharmacol Physiol — 104 subjects, 2% ectoin (PMID 17519560)
Marini et al. 2013, Skin Pharmacol Physiol — 65 patients, atopic dermatitis (PMID 23949258)

Product percentages quoted in the label chapter were read from the
manufacturers' own listings in 2026 and can change without notice. Check the
INCI on the bottle in your hand, not the number on the front.

Nothing here is medical advice, and nothing here is sponsored. No brand named
in this video paid to be in it.

Which ingredient should I take apart next — and is there one you have stopped
believing in?
```
